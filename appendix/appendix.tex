\section{Research question and scope}
This project asks whether a context-sensitive target representation distinguishes
same-sense from different-sense uses more effectively than static alternatives.
It compares type-level GloVe, a lexical GloVe context mean, and an unfine-tuned
BERT-base target representation. It is a controlled representation probe, not a
claim about fine-tuned task state of the art.

\section{Representations}
\subsection{Static baselines}
We used the uncased GloVe 6B vectors with 300 dimensions for both static
conditions. The vectors are from the
\url{https://nlp.stanford.edu/projects/glove/} release. In the target-only
condition, both sentences use the vector for the same WiC lemma. Its cosine
similarity is therefore 1 whenever GloVe contains that lemma. We found 18
training lemmas without vectors and filled those missing scores with the median
valid score from training.

For the context condition, we took word tokens on either side of the marked
target, left the target out, and averaged the available GloVe vectors. We tested
windows of two, five, and ten words on each side, plus the whole sentence. When
no context vector was available on one side, we used the lemma vector for that
side instead. This happened once per window in training and did not happen in
validation.

\subsection{Contextual target}
The contextual condition uses the pretrained
\path{google-bert/bert-base-uncased} checkpoint, available at
\url{https://huggingface.co/google-bert/bert-base-uncased}. We did not update its
weights. The tokenizer reports character positions for each word piece. We
selected the pieces that overlap the marked target, averaged their vectors, and
saved the target representation from every BERT layer. The mean of the last
four layers was one of the configurations compared on training data.

\section{Selection and evaluation protocol}
We kept model selection inside the training split. Seed 2026 produced 4,342
tuning pairs and 1,086 internal holdout pairs, with balanced labels in both.
Each candidate received a similarity threshold fitted on the tuning pairs for
macro F1. We compared candidates on the holdout pairs by macro F1, then by
accuracy and ROC-AUC if needed, with a preset simpler choice for exact ties.
After choosing the GloVe context window and BERT representation, we fitted
their final thresholds using all 5,428 training pairs. We had already checked
the validation text for data quality, but its labels were used for scoring only
after those choices were fixed.

\section{Error analysis}
The 106 pairs missed by both main systems contain 54 that the GloVe target
diagnostic also missed and 52 that it happened to answer correctly. BERT made
210 mistakes in all. Of these, 33 were very close to its threshold, within
0.025, while 53 were among the quarter with the largest distances from it.

BERT recalled 77.43\% of same-meaning pairs and 56.74\% of different-meaning
pairs. The corresponding figures for GloVe context were 46.39\% and 64.58\%.
Among our saved examples, BERT separated an anatomical \emph{ball}/\emph{balls}
pair that has the same meaning. It placed different meanings of \emph{engrave}
and \emph{map} close together. It also brought together the same meaning in
\emph{rust remover} and \emph{paint remover}. These examples help us inspect
the mistakes, but they cannot establish a cause for them.

\section{Reproducibility record}
We first ran the full BERT pass on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU.
On 29 September 2026, we ran it again on an Apple Silicon CPU using the same
WiC release, GloVe vectors, and pinned BERT revision. All 638 primary
predictions and the reported intervals matched the saved run. The cosine
scores had small hardware-level numerical differences. The repository includes
our locked dependencies, configuration, commands, and saved results.

\section{Limitations}
This experiment uses one English benchmark and one frozen BERT base model. The
bootstrap intervals vary the sampled validation pairs; they do not cover every
choice we made while designing the study. The GloVe average leaves out word
order and syntax, and one cutoff may hide useful differences between words.
The unpublished test labels prevented us from calculating a test result.
